## 1. Candidate Problem  
`20 pts`

> Two sentences. One problem from your own experience: your major, a job, a campus
> process, a family business, a team. Pick something you actually care about.

*When I worked as an intern in my freshman year summer, I helped draft a research proposal that had to do with technology our company worked closely with. The paper was vehemently denied by a single reviewer who seemed personally against the conclusions of the paper.*

---

## 2. Wickedness Test  
`25 pts`

> Evaluate your problem against at least FOUR characteristics from Session 1A.
> One short paragraph or four labeled bullets. End with an honest verdict.
>
> Available characteristics: hard to formulate a problem statement; no stopping rule;
> solutions are good or bad rather than true or false; no immediate or ultimate test;
> one-shot solutions with no trial and error; no exhaustively describable solution set;
> may be a symptom of another problem; the discrepancy has multiple valid explanations;
> the problem solver is liable for consequences.
>
> "This is actually a tame problem" is a valid verdict when the reasoning is sound.

*It is not easy to come up with a problem statement for this issue, since we were unsure whether or not there was an issue with the paper, or the reviewer had a personal vendetta against it. There was no immediate and ultimate test for this problem, because sending the paper back to the same publication didn't seem like an option. We considered sending it to another publication, but that is not an immediate solution to the problem at hand. There was a lack of trial and error opportunities, since the odds of a publication accepting your paper after revoking it once are slim, and all rejection occurences are listed in the publication when the paper gets published. The publishers of the paper, or those with their name on it, were liable for the consequences of the solution. The reviewer had issues with the honesty of the paper, as he believed the paper's ties to our company may have biased the framing of the paper. This means that any actions taken to change the opinion on our paper may not be a fully honest and scientific paper.*

**Verdict: I believe it has the qualities of a WICKED problem.**

## 3. Three Framings  
`30 pts`

> Three DISTINCT problem statements for the same situation. The test: a reader should
> not be able to swap two of them without changing what would get built.

**Framing A (the obvious one):**
The single reviewer's disapproval of the content of the paper prevents us from presenting the results of this scientific paper to the world. 
**Framing B (stakeholder shift):**
Reviewers cannot verify the integrity of scientific papers with corporate ties without complete access to all records of the paper.
**Framing C (goal shift):**
Industry led research has has trouble proving credibility when authors are tied to companies that could benefit from the findings of the paper.
---

## 4. Commit and Justify  
`included in Framings`

> Pick one framing. Defend it in a short paragraph. Then name what your choice makes
> invisible. Every framing forecloses something.

**Chosen framing:**
The obvious one.
**Why:**
From the perspective of what would be most beneficial to us, the creators of the paper, choosing to view the reviewer as an obstacle and not a valid critic leads us to a solution that makes it the most likely that the paper gets published.
**What this framing makes invisible:**
It makes the possibility that the conflict of interest present in the making of this paper is not as up front and honest as you would want from a scientific paper.

## 5. Oath Check  
`15 pts`

**The tool I already assumed:**
A simple word document
**What would have to be true for it to be justified:**
The core methodology of the paper would have to be airtight and unquestionable. The primary goal is publishing a peer-reviewed paper, settling for a white paper or any other alternative publication is not an answer. Finally, we must make sure that the potential conflicts of interest present in the authors and the corporation backing the paper are up front and honest when submitting for publication.
